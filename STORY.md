# TITANS — story, quests, progression, exploration (DRAFT for discussion)

Status: thinking, not building (user, 2026-10-31: "don't work on it yet but start thinking story
and quests and progression and exploration"). Nothing here is decided. Where there is a choice, the
options are laid out with a recommendation; PLAN.md stays the plan of record until the user picks.

The feeling it all has to serve (PLAN.md §2c): dread through scale, weather, light and sound —
never jumpscares. Accomplishment, not survival. Small person, enormous world.

## 1. The story

### What we already have in the world (the story should explain it)
- Giants three hundred metres tall, walking on their own, leaving lakes; sleeping ones become hills.
- The First Ones' works: the Sentinel kneeling with its sword, the Undercroft, the Colonnade, the
  Great Stair and the sealed Gate, chained stones hanging in the air.
- A fallen First One's ribs and skull in the Dune Sea.
- Striders and leviathans that never come near; colossi kilometres tall beyond the mountains.
- A low cloud deck that the tall things vanish into, and storms.

### The central mystery (from the original spec): the First Ones were running from something.

**Option A — The sky is coming down.** *(recommended)*
The cloud deck is not weather. It is lower than it used to be, and it is still descending. The
First Ones built upward to hold it off (the Colonnade's pillars reached the clouds once; the stones
were chained so they would not be pulled up into it). The colossi beyond the mountains are not
standing — they are *holding*. One of them fell: the bones in the Dune Sea. The giants are waking
because the sky has come low enough to touch their heads. The striders and leviathans live up in
it; they are not monsters, they are what lives in a sky.
- Why it works: every scary image the user showed is already this — things cut off by cloud.
  Weather stops being decoration and becomes the antagonist without anything ever attacking you.
- The ending: you climb the Eldest above the cloud deck for the first time in the game, and see
  what is up there. (What exactly is the last big decision — §1.4.)

**Option B — The giants are sleepwalking home.** The giants walk because something calls them to
the Gate; when they arrive, the world ends (or begins again). You decide whether to open it.
- Quieter, sadder; less tied to the weather.

**Option C — You are one of them.** The wanderer is a First One's child, tiny, and grows across
the game — by the end, as tall as a giant. Strong progression fantasy, but it spends the
megalophobia: the player stops being small.

### Who you are
A small figure in a red cloak who wakes at the bottom of the Undercroft, rain falling through a
skylight onto your face. No name. You carry a lantern that does not need oil. The Sentinel's hood
is the first thing you see when you climb out — and over the game you learn it is facing the
valley because it was the last guard, and it is the reason the cloud is highest here.

### How it is told (no cutscene dumps, no LLM)
- **Echoes**: stones the First Ones left (about 30), each a few lines, found in hard places.
- **The world changing** as you act: bells push the cloud back over a land; a woken giant walks a
  new path; the Gate opens.
- **Wordless moments**: a giant stopping to look at you; a strider stepping over the plaza while you
  are in the Undercroft and its foot comes down through a skylight.

### The ending (open — pick later)
- Above the cloud: the colossi beyond the mountains are holding up something enormous, and one of
  their places is empty. Do you take the Eldest there, or ring the last bell and let the sky fall
  slowly, giving the giants their rest? (Two endings; no wrong one.)

## 2. The main quest — The Three Bells (refined)

The plan of record already has "The Three Bells". With Option A it becomes:

| Chapter | Where | What you do | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue | The Undercroft, the valley | Wake, climb the stair, find the lantern, follow its light to the Sentinel; climb its knee and touch the glyph on the sword | The lantern now points to the next place; the first giant turns its head to look at you |
| I. The Wood | The Old Wood | The bell tower is choked by the roots of a giant tree. Wake the sleeping moss giant (climb it, its brow glyph); it walks, and its footsteps flood the hollow and lift the roots | Ringing the bell lifts the cloud over the Wood: sun in the forest for the first time |
| II. The Dunes | The Dune Sea | The bell hangs in the Fallen's ribcage, high up. Light braziers to bend a walking giant's path under the ribs; ride it; leap across | The cloud lifts over the dunes; the Fallen's skull is seen to have been looking at the sky |
| III. The Steps | The Steps | Climb the Colonnade's tallest pillar into the cloud deck (the first time inside it: white-out, the striders' legs passing close) to reach the bell at its top | The Gate unseals |
| The Eldest | Beyond the Gate | The largest giant in the game. The final climb takes you through the cloud deck and above it | The ending |

Each chapter teaches one thing: climb a giant, ride a giant, climb into the cloud.

## 3. Side quests and things to find

- **Echoes** (lore, ~30): the First Ones' story in fragments. Each also lengthens your grip (the
  SotC lizard tails idea: exploration directly makes climbing better).
- **Viewpoints**: high places with a cairn. Reach one, light it: that part of the map is revealed
  and its places named. (The map is already revealed by climbing high — see §5.)
- **Giant moments**: a giant drinking at a lake; two giants passing each other; one lying down to
  sleep. Witnessing them is noted in the journal. No reward but the sight.
- **Shrines** (small, wordless puzzles): pressure plates you need a giant's footfall to press; a
  sun-shaft through a skylight that must be reflected; a bell you can only reach by riding a strider's
  shin as it swings.
- **The leviathans' shadows**: following one across a land leads to where it drops something.
- **Names**: every giant has one, carved somewhere in the world; find it and the journal knows it.

## 4. Progression (no levels, no stats screen)

- **Grip** — grows with Echoes found. The main measure of how far you can go.
- **Tools, from the story**:
  - the **Lantern** (prologue): its beam points to the current goal; lighting braziers.
  - the **Horn** (chapter I): call a nearby giant; it turns toward you, or walks to you.
  - the **Cloak** (chapter II): glide from heights — the world's verticality opens up.
  - **Pitons** (chapter III): drive one in to rest anywhere on a climb (grip refills slowly).
- **The world itself**: lands cleared of cloud, giants that know you, the map filling in.

## 5. Exploration

- **See it, go there**: every land has one thing visible from far away (the Sentinel, the Ribs,
  the Colonnade, the giant trees, …), and the next thing is visible from the top of that one.
- **Height reveals**: the map uncovers around you, more the higher you stand. Climbing is
  exploring.
- **No markers by default**: the lantern's beam for the main quest; nothing else on screen.
- **Secrets in the vertical**: the tops of pillars, the insides of skulls, the backs of giants,
  the floor of the Undercroft.
